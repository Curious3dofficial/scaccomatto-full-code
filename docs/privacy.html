Scaccomatto

Privacy

Last updated: November 2026. This covers Scaccomatto version 1.0.

Short version: you can play without an account, and then everything stays on your computer except what an internet game needs to pass between the two players. If you make an account, we keep only what the account needs, and you can delete it any time. Nothing tracks you.

What the app keeps on your computer

  • Your settings, achievements and the guest name you play under (something like "Guest482913").
  • Your Game Library, your last analysis and any player pictures you add, in a folder called .scaccomatto in your home folder.
  • Problem reports, if something goes wrong, in .scaccomatto/logs. These never leave your computer unless you choose to send one to us.

Playing someone on your network

When you host or join a game on the same Wi-Fi or network, the two computers talk to each other directly. What gets sent is only what the game needs: your guest name, your moves and any chat messages you type. None of it goes through us.

Playing someone over the internet

Internet games are passed between the two players through Google Firebase, a service we use for this.

  • The first time you host or join a game, the app gets a random guest number from Firebase. There's no name, email or account behind it, and unused guest numbers are deleted after 30 days.
  • While a game is waiting for an opponent, Firebase holds its code, variant and clock.
  • During the game it holds your guest name, your moves and any chat messages, just long enough to pass them on. Each message is removed once the other player has it, and the whole game is deleted when it ends.
  • Like any website, Google's servers see your IP address when the app talks to them.

We don't look at games or chat, and we don't keep them.

If you make an account

Accounts are optional and run on Google Firebase. We keep:

  • Your email address, to sign you in and to reset your password. It's never shown to other players. If you sign in with Google, we get your Google email and name instead.
  • Your username, profile name and picture (if you add one).
  • Your rating and your wins, losses and draws from rated games.
  • Your settings and achievements, so they follow you to another computer.
  • Which rated games have already been counted.

Other signed-in players can see your username, name, picture and rating, for example on the leaderboard or when you play them. Your password is handled by Google; we never see it.

Deleting your account

Settings > Privacy > Delete account removes your profile, picture, settings, achievements and sign-in for good. The only thing left is an unlinked list of game codes already counted, so the same game can't be counted twice.

Friends

If you add friends, we keep your friends list and any requests waiting for an answer. While the game is open, your friends can see that you're online, and whether you're in a game. Set Online status to Nobody in Settings > Privacy to stop that. A challenge to a friend keeps the game's code, variant and clock until it's answered or two minutes pass. Profile visibility decides whether players who aren't your friends see your picture, join date and record, and whether you're on the leaderboard.

Blocking and reporting

Your block list stays on your computer, and in your account if you have one. If you report someone, the report comes to us with the reason, your note, and the moves and chat from that game, so we can check what happened. Only we can read reports, and the player you reported isn't told who reported them. We keep a report until we've dealt with it, and for up to a year after if the player was banned. A banned player can't play online, change their profile or be rated.

Who can make an account

You need to be at least 13, or the age your country sets for agreeing to this yourself, if that's higher.

What we don't do

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking.
  • Nothing about you is sold or shared.

This website

This page is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you open it, GitHub may log your IP address for security, as described in GitHub's own privacy statement. Its fonts load from Google Fonts, and the download buttons ask GitHub which installer is the newest; both see your IP address the way any website you visit does. The page itself stores nothing about you.

Removing everything

Uninstall Scaccomatto, then delete the .scaccomatto folder in your home folder.

If this changes

If a future version needs to keep anything more, this page will say exactly what and why before it does.
